Advanced guide

Work through difficult genealogy questions with a clear method.

Advanced research is careful reasoning: build a case from many small, well-documented clues.

  1. Study the locality as closely as the person. Learn county boundaries, nearby towns, churches, courts, and migration routes for the period you are researching.
  2. Use indirect evidence when a direct record is absent. Neighbors, witnesses, land transactions, probate files, and naming patterns can reveal family networks.
  3. Separate facts from conclusions in your notes. Explain why each source supports—or does not support—a relationship or event.
  4. Write a short proof summary before changing the tree: the question, the evidence, conflicts, and your conclusion. Revisit it when new records appear.
